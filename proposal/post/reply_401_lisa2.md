This is part of a project at GML's Carbon Cycle Greenhouse Gases group to get UFS-Chem conserving trace gases over decadal scales. We're starting from UFS-Chem v1 (He et al., 2026, JAMES: the UFS Weather Model with CATChem and the GFDL AM4.1 gas-phase chemistry), with the GFS v17 P8 physics (`FV3_GFS_v17_p8_ugwpv1`, `progsigma` = .true.). I have a suite of 37 changes that get us there, which I'm sending upstream as small PRs as I verify them; the notes, patches and measurements are in [pera-noaa/ufs-water-conservation](https://github.com/pera-noaa/ufs-water-conservation). Some of them are plain bug fixes that make this instability show up more often. For example, [SA1](https://github.com/pera-noaa/ufs-water-conservation/blob/main/proposal/water_closure_proposal.md#1-convection-fix-sa1): SAMF's cloud-base correction subtracts a flux-limited value rather than what the updraft actually took. That creates about 0.8 % of convective rain and makes convectively transported tracers drift by up to 0.3 %/yr. With it, `hafs_global_1nest_atm` overshoots 1.7 times as often, and with the whole suite it crashed within 6 h.

So you're right that our own configuration, with `progsigma` = .true., should not see it. We need the fix because `hafs_global_1nest_atm` runs with `progsigma` = .false. in the regression tests, and it crashes there on develop too, within 48 h. For `progsigma` = .true., our only test is `hafs_global_1nest_atm` with it switched on (10 members, 48 h): no layer heats by more than 4 K in a step, with or without the floor, and the floor changes precipitation by +0.1 % (Christian's 2a95266 by +0.3 %). We have not tested the GFS v17 coupled configuration, or forecast skill.
